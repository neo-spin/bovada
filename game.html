A Guide to Special Olympics Sports

Welcome to your resource for the world of Special Olympics. We'll walk you through the sports, the spirit of competition, and the mission of inclusion.

Celebrating AbilitiesGlobal UnityFor Every Athlete

Understanding the Game

Whether you are new to the movement or a lifelong supporter, there is always something new to learn. Special Olympics sports are adapted to allow athletes of all ability levels to compete and showcase their skills.

Deep Dive into the Sports

Let's break down the key aspects of some of the most popular sports in the Special Olympics.

Athletics (Track & Field)

The Events: From sprints and long-distance running to shot put and long jump, Athletics is one of the most popular sports.

The Spirit: It showcases the fundamental skills of running, jumping, and throwing, embodying the motto "Let me be brave in the attempt."

Must Watch: The 100-meter dash and the 4x100m relay are always thrilling events, full of speed and teamwork.

Swimming

The Events: Athletes compete in a wide range of events, including freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke, and relays.

The Spirit: Swimming offers a unique sense of freedom and accomplishment, providing a full-body workout that improves cardiovascular health.

Must Watch: The Unified relay events are a perfect example of inclusion, as athletes with and without intellectual disabilities compete together.

Bocce

The Goal: A game of strategy and skill, the objective is to roll your bocce balls closer to the target ball (the pallina) than your opponent.

Why It's Great: Bocce is a truly inclusive sport, accessible to athletes of all ages and abilities. It promotes social interaction and strategic thinking.

Unified Sports®

The Goal: To build a more inclusive world by having people with and without intellectual disabilities play on the same team.

Why It's Great: It breaks down stereotypes about people with intellectual disabilities in a fun, powerful way. It's a model for inclusion in all aspects of life.

How to Get Involved

You can be a part of the inclusion revolution! Opportunities are available to volunteer, coach, or simply be a "fan in the stands" to cheer on the athletes. Every role makes a difference.
